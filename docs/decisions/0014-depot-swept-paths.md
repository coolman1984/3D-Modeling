# 0014: Vehicle depot pack and swept-path manoeuvres

**Date:** 2026-09-25 · **Status:** accepted

## Context

T9 (plan §7): vehicle profiles (length, width, height, wheelbase, overhangs, turning radius,
reverse allowed), bays (square, angled, parallel; parking, maintenance, wash, charge), gates;
Dubins / Reeds-Shepp paths; the swept body checked against walls, columns, parked vehicles and
zones; "a bay is usable only if the vehicle can enter and leave".

## Decisions

| Topic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicles | Item types of category `vehicle`, `meta`: wheelbase, overhangs, `turnCircle` (kerb-to-kerb diameter, what spec sheets give), reverse. Planning radius of the rear axle R = √((D/2)² − wheelbase²) − width/2 | People enter spec-sheet numbers; the conversion is explicit and tested |
| Bays and gates | Zones: `bay` (meta vehicle, use, heading, entry nose-in / backed-in / either) and `gate` (meta heading = driving in). `bayRow` generates rows (90°, 60°, 45°, parallel) | Bays are painted areas, not objects: parked vehicles are items and do clash |
| Paths | `packages/industry`: Dubins shortest forward paths (six words, Shkel & Lumelsky closed forms) and the same driven wholly in reverse; a hybrid-A* search (Dolgov et al.) over full-lock-left / straight / full-lock-right arcs, forward and — when allowed — reverse with a gear-change cost, finishing with an exact Dubins shot. Full lock and straight only: Reeds & Shepp showed shortest paths use only these | Deterministic; handles three-point turns and backing out; the full Reeds-Shepp analytic set (48 words) is not needed for the question "can it get there" |
| Body check | Discs of radius half the width + 3 cm along the body's length (inside its ends), plus points on both ends and corners, read against an exact distance field on a 5 cm grid with the sampling slack. Overstated by at most ~11 cm a side and ~6 cm at the ends; pinned by tests (a 1.8 m car fits a 2.0 m lane, not 1.9 m; nose 15 cm from a wall fits) | Conservative (never says "fits" when it does not) but tight enough for real bays; a first version with plain discs overstated the ends by 70 cm and would have rejected nose-to-nose rows |
| Access | For each bay: from any gate into the parked pose, and from there out to any gate facing out, with the bay's own vehicle; items standing in the bay are ignored while it is checked. A coarse (25 cm) connectivity test for something slightly narrower than the vehicle proves "no way" quickly; otherwise the search either finds a way or gives up after 25 000 states → **unknown**, never "no" and never "yes" | Missing proof is unknown, as everywhere |
| Cost | Answers are memoised on everything they depend on (walls, obstacles, zones, items, item types, the bay) — a test proves a resized item type changes the answer. The editor checks the saved plan, not each frame of a drag | 5 car bays ≈ 0.3 s; a 12 m bus turning round in a small yard ≈ 2.5 s |
| Rules | bay access, bay size (vehicle width + 2 × 30 cm, length + 25 cm; common guidance), headroom (tallest bay vehicle + 20 cm; unknown without a ceiling), gates ≥ 1 | Each names its source |
| UI | Bays panel (usable / no way / not settled per bay, bay-row generator, gates), the chosen bay's swept path on the plan (body every metre and the rear-axle path, in and out), vehicle model in 3D, report cover | Plan §9: T9 swept-path overlay |

## Consequences

- Big vehicles in tight yards are slow (seconds) and may come back "not settled"; a faster search
  (precomputed primitives, a Reeds-Shepp heuristic) is the next step if fleets are large.
- Not modelled yet: articulated vehicles (trailers), one-way lanes, lane markings that are not
  walls, overhangs sweeping over low kerbs, drivers' preferred manoeuvres.
- Bays generated by the panel are rectangles; any zone with a heading works for access.
